Washi tape — edge treatments

8 cases · 1 control, 1 real reference, 6 sourced mechanisms ← lab index source notes
Read this before judging. You asked for serrated edges like the tape was taken out from a roller. The one real washi asset found on a live site measures as a tear, not a serration: its ends wander ±6px on a 116px width in two or three broad lobes, where a dispenser blade would give ten to twenty alternations. Both targets are drawn below — C is the roller cut you asked for, R / D / F / G are what real tape measures as. Picking is yours.

Three things about our current strip are wrong independently of the tips, all measured, all in the table at the bottom: it carries a 1px border that no real tape has, it is axis-aligned where the real one sits at 5.42° to its own edge, and its fill is an opaque two-tone repeat where the sourced stripe has a transparent third band. Case E tests the fill alone, with square ends, so you can see which problem is which.

R · gneiss.place, its actual asset at its actual rule

Source: gneiss.place/nonhtml/css/deco.css, selector .taped::before, asset /assets/images/tape/tape1.png. Read from the site's own served CSS 2026-08-22.
Premise check that nearly failed: the rule is not in index.css, which the earlier note named — that file has zero occurrences of "tape". It is in deco.css, one of six @imports. The citation was right and the file reference was wrong.
Verbatim geometry: height:50px; width:150px; background-size:contain; top:-20px; left:40%. Only the file path is changed here.
Why it cannot just be adopted: it is a photograph of one physical tape in one colourway. D27 needs four section colours out of one definition and a photo cannot recolour. That is what F and G are for.

C · serrated — two-gradient mask sawtooth (the roller cut you asked for)

Source: CSS-Tricks, Stuart Langridge, 2020-05-12. His tooth verbatim: linear-gradient(to bottom right, white, white 50%, transparent 50%, transparent).
The trap his article exists to document: the sawtooth alone cannot be the mask — confining it with mask-size leaves the rest of the element unmasked and therefore invisible. It has to be two mask images, tooth plus a core covering everything else.
Ours, not his: the serration is transposed to the two ends rather than the bottom edge, because that is where a blade cuts tape; and the core gradient's stops are ours, since the article gives that half in prose only. His pens are 403 to this session, so no pen is cited — only the article body.
Pitch 6px = roughly a tenth of the reference's 116px width scaled to our 26px strip. Arithmetic on a measured number. The most obvious bench slider on the page.

Sourced

A · torn — feTurbulence + feDisplacementMap, two primitives

Source: Daniel Jones, "How to make rough edges with CSS and SVGs", corroborated by bengammon.co.uk which credits it. Verbatim: feTurbulence type="fractalNoise" baseFrequency=".05" numOctaves="4" then feDisplacementMap in="SourceGraphic" scale="4", applied as filter:url(#squiggle).
The one change, and the arithmetic for it: scale is in user units, so it does not scale with the strip. His 4 on our 26px tape is ~4.5x proportionally rougher than on the 116px reference. 4 × (26/116) = 0.90, used as 1.
Cost: it roughens all four edges, not just the ends. On paper tape that is arguably correct, but it changes the long edges too — which means it moves how the strip meets the card, and that is a layout question, not only a look.
Also: one filter id = one tear shape shared by every strip on the page unless each gets its own seed.

B · torn — TornPaper.js chain, edge half only

Source: TornPaper.js v0.0.3, Wakana Y.K./happy358, MIT, read from tornpaper.js itself rather than the README.
The three primitives that make this a tear and not a wobble, which A does not have: feGaussianBlur 0.5 feathers the alpha first so the edge has fibre; feMorphology erode eats inward so the tear can bite in rather than only bulge out; feOffset shifts it back so the erosion is asymmetric. Its xChannelSelector="B" yChannelSelector="G" pair takes x and y from different noise channels so the edge wanders in two axes.
REMIX because a subset was taken: its feTurbulence + feDiffuseLighting half paints paper grain, and D27 removed paper texture from the cards. Keeping it would reverse a decision, so it is dropped.
Scaled, with the arithmetic: erode 5 → 1, offset 2 → 0, tornScale 10 → 2.2, all ×(26/116). Their 5px erode on a 26px strip would eat 38% of its width.

D · torn — irregular clip-path, their pitch on our axis

Source: winterwind.com/tutorials/css/29, a 21-point clip-path: polygon().
The numbers are the point. Their points sit at 1, 4, 9, 13, 19, 21, 26, 30, 36, 43, 50, 52, 61, 69, 70, 76, 81, 84, 91, 97, 99 percent — irregular spacing. That is the whole difference between "torn" and "zigzag" in a polygon; a uniform pitch reads as a postage stamp.
Ours: theirs runs along a horizontal strip painted in the background colour over the sheet. That works on a flat ground and cannot work here, where a live scene shows through. So it becomes a clip on the tape itself, with their pitch numbers as the y positions and their 0-5% / 94-100% amplitude as the x. Their numbers, our axis.
Cheapest of the three tear routes — no filter, no rasterisation, no asset.

Sourced

E · the FILL, not the ends — three-stop stripe with a transparent band

Source: Coding Artist, "How To Create Washi Tape Notes With Pure CSS". Verbatim period: repeating-linear-gradient(45deg, C 0, C 5px, transparent 5px, transparent 10px, D 10px, D 15px).
This is the citation our stripe has been missing. washi-tape.css already builds a 45° repeating gradient and had no source for that choice. Theirs is closer than ours: three stops over 15px with a transparent band, so the card shows through every third band. Ours is a two-tone opaque repeat over 10px, which is exactly why it reads as a painted bar.
Ends deliberately left square so the fill is the only variable. If E already fixes most of "looks fake", the tips were never the main problem — worth knowing before we spend a tune on them.

Remix — ours

F · the real silhouette as a MASK, so the stripe shows through it

Shape: gneiss.place tape1.png, unaltered. Idea: ours. Nothing on gneiss.place uses the asset as a mask — labelled at the moment it is added, per the rule that an invention must not inherit a source's credibility.
What it buys: the exact torn silhouette of a real tape, recolourable into all four D27 section colours, because the PNG carries only alpha here.
What it costs: an asset, and every strip on the site gets the same tear. Real tape is torn differently every time. Rotating or flipping the mask per placement is the obvious mitigation and it is not built here.

G · F, plus the two other measured differences

F, with no border and the tape's own 5.42°. Measured off tape1.png: the tape is drawn at 5.42° off its own bitmap axis, so it is not parallel to the card edge it sits on. Our .taped rotates the card; nothing has ever rotated the strip. Using the measured angle is transcription. Picking a different one would not be, so it is not done here.
If G is the winner, the fix list is: drop the border, rotate the strip, mask the shape — and the tips were only one third of the problem.
