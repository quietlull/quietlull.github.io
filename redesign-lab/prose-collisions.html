2 · inside .prose — today
.prose a (0,1,1) ties .co-ref a (0,1,1), and decisions.css loads AFTER the component sheets on all six pages, so the tie goes to prose. The cancelled hairline comes back. And the hover splits between the two rules, which is the part worth seeing: the component still wins background (it declares the shorthand, so the fill stays transparent) while prose wins color and sets it to the near-black panel token. Dark text on a dark panel. Measured at 1.06:1.
3 · OPTION A — scope it under .prose
Every component rule gains a .prose prefix: .prose .co-ref a (0,2,1) beats .prose a (0,1,1). Cost: the component now only works inside a post. The same callout on the projects or ramblings page loses its styling entirely, because there is no .prose wrapper there.
4 · OPTION B — the component raises itself
The component doubles its own class: .co-ref.co-ref a (0,2,1) also beats .prose a, and mentions prose nowhere. Cost: it reads as a trick and every future component has to remember to do it. Gain: the component stays portable and works identically in a post, on projects, and on the bench.
A correction the demo produced. This failure has been written up as "gold text on gold fill". It is not: measured, the fill stays transparent and the TEXT goes near-black, 1.06:1 against the panel. The cause is a per-property split, which only shows up when you render it. Fixed in the notes.

They render identically in boxes 3 and 4 — that is the point. Both fixes work. The decision is not which looks better, it is which rule the other 52 losing declarations get rewritten to follow, and whether a component is allowed to be prose-only. Pick one and it applies to all eight components; picking neither leaves box 2.
Why any of this happens. On all six final pages the component stylesheets load AFTER decisions.css, but load order only breaks ties. A .prose a is (0,1,1) and a component's .rc is (0,1,0), so the prose rule wins outright no matter what order they arrive in. Every fix is the same shape and that is why it is one decision rather than nine: the component either raises its own specificity, or it gets scoped under .prose too. Picking one and applying it everywhere is the call. An equal-specificity tie is listed too, because there the load order decides and that is fragile for a different reason.
