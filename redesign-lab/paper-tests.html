A · the EDGE is paper · gneiss.place border-image

Their .navbox has no drawn border - its edge IS a torn-paper photograph, sliced with border-image using a fill slice, an asymmetric width and a -0.1deg rotation so nothing sits perfectly square. Same site as the washi tape, so the page would speak one material language. The hatch you see is a stand-in for the photographed edge.

B · the GROUND is paper · gneiss.place tiled surface

A photographed paper surface, tiled, scaled well off 100% so it never reads as a tile. The stain is a separate ::before at opacity .25, which means one paper can wear many different marks - that separation is the good idea in it. Ruling is drawn as geometry here, which is the honest stand-in for a tiled photograph.

C · the SURFACE is paper · ibelick feTurbulence, verbatim

One inline feTurbulence as a data URI, quoted exactly from ibelick.com: baseFrequency .65, numOctaves 3, opacity .12, background-size 182px. No image request, resolution independent, monochrome by construction so it needs no palette exception. This is the real thing, not a stand-in. Honest limit: it is film grain, so it gives the card tooth rather than fibre or a deckle. It can also sit UNDER A or B rather than compete.

the sheet we bakedas the shader combines them

E · BOTH sheets, washi tile 1 + cold press tile 4

The combination the scene actually ships, per CONFIG.lanterns.paper: washi at tile 1 carrying the large fibre, cold press at tile 4 inside it as tooth. The shader combines them with SUBTRACT so the sheets cancel where they agree instead of reading as more noise; exclusion is the CSS blend that behaves closest to that. If the cards and the scene should feel like the same paper, this is the one that matches.
