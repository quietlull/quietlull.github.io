Achievement tile + locked state

6 versions · tile 88 x 31 · locked / empty state 641 x 40 · measured off about-blockout.html?v=panels at 1440 · V1-V3 unchanged, V2 is the one to beat back to lab index
This builds the TILE and the LOCKED state. It does not build the wall. You said the trophy wall has no source and needs a design conversation first, so nothing here proposes an arrangement. The 7 tiles in each stage are the blockout's own EXPLORER group at its measured pitch, because a tile cannot be judged at any other size.

The boxes are measurements, not labels. Read off about-blockout.html?v=panels rendered at viewport 1440 with getBoundingClientRect this session: locked / empty state 641 x 40, tile 88 x 31, wall flex-wrap with gap:10px, 6 tiles per row. The 641 is .main 675 minus the panel's 4px of border and 30px of padding. The blockout's own prose says 645; it renders 641, and the rendered value wins.

One finding constrains V1-V5. At 88 x 31 no title fits: after the icon slot and the padding there are about 50px left, which is five characters, against real titles like "Behind the Curtain" and "Hey, You Touched My Fly!". So the tile is icon only everywhere and the name is the accessible name, not visible text. That is consistent with where 88 x 31 comes from: dimden's own button is an image, with no text layout inside it. V6 breaks this on purpose and says so: the finding is about TITLES, and a four-to-six character count is not a title. Its readout measures the difference.

Nothing invents a mystery glyph. An unearned achievement has no icon by definition, because you have not seen it. What V1-V3 actually differ on is whether the empty slot is drawn at all, and how the earned one is marked. V4-V6 move three different dials and have their own lede further down.

A citation already in this repo needed correcting. about-blockout.html:236 says the badge wall is dimden's #webtiles-container "at true size: 88x31 tiles, gap 10". Fetched live this session, their gap:10px is verbatim, but their container has no flex-wrap (it holds a 250x270 iframe beside a paragraph) and there is no 88x31 rule anywhere in their CSS. 88 x 31 is the intrinsic size of the button image they serve at /services/images/88x31.gif. So the size is sourced to an asset, the gap to a rule, and the wrapping wall is ours. Written down rather than repeated.

Not rebuilt here: the unlock toast is already picked (dimden .box at 200 x 76, achievement-tests.html .t-pick), and the tag / meta chips are decisions.css .d-tag and .d-meta, which are a different job.
The dashed outline behind every element is its measured reservation. Rendered sizes below each stage are measured live in your browser, not asserted.

    Versions 4-6 were added after reading five real achievement systems end to end
    (Minecraft, Steam, RetroAchievements, PlayStation, Xbox). V1-V3 are untouched - you
    prefer V2 and it stays as the thing to beat.

        

    Why three more, in one line. V1, V2 and V3 all move the same dial: how much CHROME a
    locked slot gets. The systems that actually ship this differ on three dials, and only one of
    them was being turned here.

        

    axis A how LOCKED reads - dimmed / hue-swapped / partly filled / absent.
    axis B is RARITY encoded - not at all / by colour / by SILHOUETTE.
    axis C what METADATA rides along - nothing / a count / a percentage / a date.

        

    V4 moves A. V5 moves B. V6 moves C. Each holds the other two still, so they can be
    judged one variable at a time and against V2.

        

    A provenance warning that has to be read before the versions. Minecraft has
    no stylesheet. Its advancement screen is drawn by the game from PNG sprites, so there
    is no CSS to transcribe and none is invented. What is cited is the decoded sprite files and
    the vanilla advancement JSON, with their repos named. Every declaration in V4 and V5 is
    ours expressing their mechanism, and both are tiered Remixed on that basis. Steam and
    RetroAchievements are the opposite case: both have real stylesheets and both were fetched, so
    V6 quotes actual declarations.

        

    The 29 are real. Every tile below is one of the achievements in
    _javascript/modules/components/achievements.js: Explorer 7, Reader 6,
    Interactor 8, Secret 3, Meta 5. The earned set is the blockout's own 14 of 29, and the
    progress numbers come from a single consistent state vector rather than being picked one at a
    time: unlocked 14, tagsRead 3, lanternKnocks 31, fireworkCount 18, avatarHoverTime 6.
  
        


  
        Version 4 · hue swap  (axis A: how locked reads)
        
  
        
    Tier: Remixed. Idea origin: theirs (Minecraft) - MECHANISM ONLY, there is
    no CSS.
        
    Source, and what kind of source it is: the advancement UI has no stylesheet, so the
    sprites were decoded instead - gui/sprites/advancements/box_obtained.png and
    box_unobtained.png from
    github.com/InventivetalentDev/minecraft-assets @1.21.4. Both are 200 x 26,
    nine-slice, border 10. The geometry is byte-identical between the two states. Only four
    colours change: #036A96 / #0489C1 / #012E40 against
    #B98F2C / #DBA213 / #493606, both over a black outline.
        
    The finding: that is a change of HUE, not of brightness. Both plates are saturated and
    sit at near-equal luminance. Of the five systems read, Minecraft is the only one that does
    not fade its locked state, and that is exactly why a screen of mostly-unearned
    advancements does not read as a screen of failures. Steam ships a separate
    _BW.jpg; PlayStation and Xbox dim client-side; all three make a wall of grey.
        
    Ours: every declaration, because there is nothing to copy.
    The fills are translucent (their plate is opaque and would sit on top of the water), and the
    bevel is expressed as a 1px border plus two zero-blur inset shadows, which are hard
    lines and not shadows.
        
    Metadata: none. Minecraft carries a title, a description and an icon, and nothing else
    - no date, no score, no percentage. V4 keeps that. Its row does carry a criteria count,
    because theirs does.
        
    THE OPEN QUESTION, and it is the whole version: the mechanism needs two
    hues at equal strength, and the palette has exactly one non-warm hue -
    --color-accent-cool #3090a8, "the cool sky the warm lanterns hang in", which
    settings.css says to spend rarely, on the single most important mark per
    view. V4 spends it on 15 locked tiles. That is the opposite of rarely. Two warm hues at equal
    luminance would read as two golds rather than as two states, so there is no quiet fix: either
    the sky stops being rare on this one panel, or V4 loses its premise. Your call.

            Version 5 · silhouette  (axis B: rarity)
            
  
            
    Tier: Remixed. Idea origin: theirs (Minecraft frames, mechanism only) +
    theirs (RetroAchievements, real CSS).
            
    Source 1, mechanism only: Minecraft's three frame sprites -
    task_frame, goal_frame, challenge_frame - are
    all 26 x 26. Rarity is carried entirely by what the outline does inside that box: task
    is a plain rect, goal chamfers, challenge steps OUT into corner spikes and IN at the waist.
    The icon well shrinks with each (400 / 368 / 382 px) and the outer box never moves.
    Rarity costs interior area, never grid space - which is the only reason a rarity system can
    exist inside a fixed 641 x 40 reservation at all.
            
    Source 2, real CSS, github.com/RetroAchievements/RAWeb
    resources/css/badge.css:
    .badgeimg { border:2px solid transparent; box-sizing:content-box } against
    .goldimage { border:2px solid gold; box-sizing:content-box } - the tier is
    only the border, and content-box keeps the art from shrinking. Their
    completion-progress.css ring adds the second axis for free: colour says which
    tier, hollow vs filled says how far. Here shape says which tier and fill says earned.
            
    The rarity is NOT invented. There is no rarity field in
    achievements.js, so rather than assigning one by taste it is read off a field
    that is already there, scope:
    section -> task (19 of 29, 66%),
    cross|site -> goal (5, 17%),
    meta -> challenge (5, 17%). Minecraft's own vanilla distribution, counted over
    all 126 advancement files in github.com/misode/mcmeta, is 72 / 8 / 20.
    Same shape, same order, and the fancy silhouette stays a minority, which is what keeps a wall
    of them calm. The mapping is ours; the field and both counts are not.
            
    Ours: the polygons. Their spikes were drawn for a 1:1 box and
    this tile is 2.84:1, so they are re-cut to sit at the four corners of 88 x 31 instead of
    being stretched sideways, and the waist pinches the left and right edges at mid-height. Also
    ours: dropping RetroAchievements' content-box border, because a
    clip-path has no border to size - the frame is a painted parent with an inset
    well, which is how the sprites are built anyway.
            
    Metadata: none on the tile. The shape is the only thing it says.
            
    The honest cost: 5 of 29 tiles get a silhouette nobody has been taught to read.
    Minecraft teaches it over hundreds of hours. A portfolio gets one visit.

                  Version 6 · the tile is the bar  (axis C: metadata)
                  
  
                  
    Tier: Remixed. Idea origin: theirs (Steam) + ROD (the count is his content
    idea, 2026-08-23).
                  
    Verbatim, from
    community.akamai.steamstatic.com/public/css/skin_1/game_achievements.css:
    .achieveFill { position:absolute; top:2px; left:2px; bottom:2px; z-index:10;
    background:#17435c } inside
    .achieveTxtHolder { border:1px solid #17435c; overflow:hidden;
    background:rgba(0,0,0,0.5) }, with the markup setting the width inline:
    <div class="achieveFill" style="width: 62%">.
                  
    The finding: the row IS the bar. The fill colour and the border colour are the same
    value, so a fully-earned thing reads as a solid plate and a barely-started one as a thin
    stub, with no second element, no track and no extra row. Minecraft does the same trick a
    different way - BetterAdvancementWidget.java computes
    floor(pct * width) and blits that many pixels from the obtained texture and the
    rest from the unobtained one, so the bar and its own background are one object.
                  
    Also from Steam, and it answers the scene: their achievement page sits over a
    full-bleed game hero and their entire answer is background: rgba(0,0,0,0.5). One
    flat scrim, no blur, one declaration. That is the precedent for using
    --color-panel here instead of reaching for a backdrop-filter.
                  
    Why this version exists. Your words, today: "maybe we can track posts written vs posts
    read and the achievements and then we can track this across design, tech art, and ramblings."
    That needs a tile that can hold a COUNT, and this is the one that can - the number goes in
    the space the icon does not use, and the progress is the tile's own background. Nothing is
    added to carry it.
                  
    The count is real data. achievements.js already ships a
    progress function on ten of the 29: well-rounded [n,5], connoisseur
    [n,10], completionist [n,15], pyrotechnician [n,50], fan-club [n,10], lantern-tapper [n,25],
    lantern-painter [n,50], lantern-master [n,100], getting-started [n,5], collector [n,15]. The
    other 19 are booleans and correctly show no number, so the version degrades on its own with no
    rule needed to switch between them.
                  
    This one breaks the icon-only finding on purpose, and the distinction matters: that
    finding was that no title fits, measured at about 50px of free width against names like
    "Behind the Curtain". A count is not a title. "31/100" is six characters, about 32px at 9px
    mono. The readout below measures it rather than asserting it.
                  
    Ours: translucent fills, because #17435c is
    opaque and this sits on a live scene; the hues; and z-index: 0 instead of their
    10, so the numeral sits above its own bar.
                  
    The honest cost: a half-filled tile says "not yet, and here is exactly how far off you
    are". That is the closest any of the six comes to pointing at something you have not got, and
    D28 scrapped the rewards precisely so nothing would. A count is not a reward, but it does
    point forward, and that is worth deciding on purpose rather than inheriting.
