3 · Procedural path jitter — the route the clock has to use

Tier: Remixed. Idea-origin theirs (rough.js, recorded in STATUS as the precedent). Written from the idea, not lifted from their source — stated plainly rather than cited as a transcription.
The mechanism: walk a path and displace each point by a small random offset, re-rolled every boil frame. The width never changes, because the path's anchor points do not move — which is precisely why the clock can use this and cannot use font swapping.

4 · The same swap, with the drift removed

Your three fonts do NOT share metrics — 55 of 68 glyphs have a different advance width, and "RODNEY FAN" drifts 5.64% (37px at 101px). Section 2 shows that raw. P100's constraint was "identical metrics or the text reflows every frame", and on a naive swap it does.

But identical metrics were never the only way. Below, every glyph gets a fixed box measured once from font 1, and the letter boils inside it. The face changes; nothing moves. Same three fonts, same swap rate, zero drift — the edge marker does not budge.

The cost, stated: the spacing becomes font 1's spacing for all three, so fonts 2 and 3 lose their own letter-fitting. On a hand-drawn face that is arguably part of the effect (a real boil wobbles the letters, not the layout), but it IS a change to how 2 and 3 were drawn, and it is your call whether that reads as boil or as squeezed.
How line boil is actually done, since it changes the advice above. The traditional recipe is three drawings, cycled in sequence, held on 2s (12 changes/sec at 24fps) or on 3s (8/sec) - which is why the rate buttons are labelled that way now. Rough Animator ships a cycle feature built on exactly this: draw 3 frames, cycle them across 21 more for a two-second boil.

So three is already the canonical count, and sequential is the canonical order - which makes my "random beats sequential" note above only half right. The reason a traditional 3-frame sequential loop does NOT read as a loop is the craft rule that comes with it: draw the three with as little deviation from each other as possible, or the boil looks too jumpy. When the drawings are near-identical the cycle reads as texture and the eye cannot find the period.

Which puts the real problem back on the fonts. Random order and per-glyph phase are digital patches for deviation that is too LARGE - Lineboil3 is 18% off on its A and 3.4% off across the line. Bring the three closer together and plain sequential-on-3s should work, the way it has for a century. Keep the toggles to compare, but the fix is in the drawings.

6 · Vertical — what the hero actually runs

The hero is not the horizontal case rotated, and treating it as one broke it. The H0 mark runs writing-mode: vertical-rl; text-orientation: upright, and two things behave differently there:

1. Raw drift is far worse — 70px, 29% of the column, against 4.26% horizontally, because the three faces have different ascent/descent. Left column below is unpinned so you can see it jump.

2. Per-glyph widths carry NO information. Under upright every glyph advances by the same em box — all ten letters measured 23.89px. So the horizontal fix, a box per glyph measured from its own width, pins nothing useful. The vertical pin is ONE uniform advance applied to every cell, taken from font 1.

And a fixed line-height does not help — measured, still 70px. In upright mode the advance comes from the font's own vertical metrics, not from line-height.
