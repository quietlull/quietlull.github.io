Section breaks — heading and section as blocks

6 sourced treatments · greybox · at the real 767px measure ← lab index source note
Your ask, P54: "make sure the headers in posts have their own spaced out cards and sections are broken down into their own bits." That is a layout change, so per the process it becomes variants rather than an edit, and each one needed a real source first. Six below.

A is your ask almost word for word — cyanilux boxes every h2, h3 and h4 and puts 30px above each one. B and C box the SECTION instead and leave the heading plain. D, E and F break sections apart with no box at all, and they are here on purpose: without them every option is a card and the page would prove the ask rather than test it.

The deciding property is probably not the radius, it is the alpha. Only dimden's panel is translucent (#000000a8, 66% black). Every other boxed option is opaque and would sit on top of the live scene instead of letting it through — on a page whose entire ground is that scene, that is the thing to judge.

No source combines a boxed heading with a boxed section. Doing both at once would be ours, so it is not drawn here. If that is what you want, say so and it gets built as a labelled combination rather than smuggled in as a transcription.

A · cyanilux — every heading IS a card

Source: cyanilux.com/css/style.css?v=12 lines 817-832, read from source 2026-08-22. Seen on /tutorials/urp-shader-code/, which carries 20 h2 on one page — this is their working system, not a one-off.
Verbatim: margin-top:30px; padding:7px 20px; background:#282828; border-left:10px solid #00aabb; border-radius:7px on h2:not(.title-no-margin):not(.title-undecorated), h3, h4.
Two details that matter more than the box: h2, h3 and h4 all get the same treatment — no size or weight escalation carries the hierarchy. And nesting is carried by FILL: a heading inside .foldout or .tabpanel goes #282828 → #303030, one step lighter per level of containment. That is a ready-made answer to the second half of your ask, drawn below as the nested block.
Greybox changes: accent #00aabb → #6f6f6f (D8, no colour), radius 7 → 0 (your locked shape rule). Everything else is theirs.
The cost, stated: their real page nests card in card in card — page shell, section, heading — three opaque fills deep before any prose. Ours has a scene behind it.

B · dimden — the SECTION is a translucent panel

This is the one that suits our page. A 66%-black panel over a live scene is exactly D27's "normal transparent cards in grey", and it comes from a site already in your gallery rather than from a rule we invented.

C · MinionsArt — a tinted block, separated by fill alone

The held note's own words: "Contrast with the black .post_card parent is tiny (#1c1c1c on #000), so the box separates by fill alone, no rule, no accent bar, no left border."
The quietest boxed option, and already effectively square at 2px. Its near-zero contrast is the interesting part: it proves a section can be a block without becoming a card.

D · Catlike Coding — numbering makes the section a unit

Source: sources/catlikecoding-prose.md — tutorials.css, verified there. Selectors body{counter-reset:h2}, h2{counter-reset:h3}, h2:before,h3:before.
The held note's own words: "Two nested CSS counters produce 1, 1.1, 1.2, 2, 2.1 automatically, and the numbers are deliberately DE-emphasised — the ::before is 14px #888 while the h2 itself is 20px #222, so the number is a small grey tick sitting before a heading that is barely larger than body text. The manuscript numbering carries the hierarchy so the type sizes never have to. No rule, no anchor icon, no colour. h2 also forces a page break in print, making the section the unit of the document."
The strongest counter-proposal to a card. It answers "sections broken down into their own bits" without drawing a single box, and it is the only one of the six whose section boundary survives printing. Also worth noting for us: Catlike is already the source behind your 94-char measure (P33), so this page and that decision come from the same site.

E · iquilezles — colour value alone, plus a bare hr

Source: sources/iquilezles-prose.md — iquilezles.org/style.css?v=2. Verbatim: h3{color:#ffffff;font-weight:normal;}h2{color:#ffffff;font-weight:normal;}, and there is no hr{} rule at all — grep -c 'hr{' returns 0, so the break is the browser's own default inset border.
Headings are DE-emphasised, not emphasised: weight reset to normal, signal is pure white against #c0c0c0 body. No size jump, no bold, no numbering, no anchor.
The transferable line from the held note: "no boxes, borders, backgrounds, radii, shadows or padding are ever used to separate a component from prose. Components differ from prose only by (a) colour, (b) font family, (c) line-height."
The floor of the range. This is what the post looks like if the answer to P54 turns out to be "no".

F · acegikmo — the heading as a ruled chapter divider

Source: sources/acegikmo-prose.md — acegikmo.com/shapes/css/styles.css.
Two devices, per the held note: h1 is a full-width section rule — centred display type with border-bottom: solid 1px and no border-color, so the rule inherits color and heading and rule move together. And h5 is display:inline, a run-in heading that flows into the paragraph beside it instead of taking its own line. Whole scale at font-weight:100, every level with a tight .3em bottom margin so the heading sticks to the text it labels.
Why the borderless trick is worth having regardless of which variant wins: one accent change moves the type and its rule together, it costs nothing, and it works under any palette — which matters while D18 keeps colour undecided.

G · the D+F mix you asked for

Rod 2026-08-22: "can we do a mix of D and F?"
D half: Catlike Coding's nested counters — body{counter-reset:h2} / h2{counter-reset:h3}, number de-emphasised at 14px #888. F half: acegikmo's ruled chapter divider — centred, font-weight:100, border-bottom: solid 1px with no border-color so the rule inherits color. Both verbatim; sources in the note.
THE COMBINATION IS OURS and is labelled that way at the moment it is added — no source does both. What makes them merge rather than collide: neither uses SIZE to carry hierarchy. Catlike de-emphasises the number, acegikmo de-emphasises the type. So the counter can do the ranking and the rule can do the separation while the type stays quiet.
One judgement call, flagged: the subsection keeps its number but not the rule. A rule under every h3 turns the page into a ladder, and F's divider is a chapter device. Say if you want the rule on both levels.
Free bonus from F: the borderless border-bottom means one accent change moves the type and its rule together — useful while D18 keeps colour undecided.
