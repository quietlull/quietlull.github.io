Snatchems was an unfinished project that i worked on with a team of in a team of 9 we went though a couple major revisions and added some new features which changed the game several times early on i worked mostly on Art and Dialogue writing for NPC characters. Later i worked on some of the major features during the aforementioned revisions. 

# Early Design
Early on Snatchems was meant to be a FPS , where you would platform between levels and explore a few 3D worlds talking to NPC's and fighting through hordes of enemies to collect these creatures called snatchems. In this version snatchems were purely decorative you would be able to decorate your digital desktop with them raising them and interacting with them or having them interact with each other kind of like Tamagotchi. 

In our first demo we had NPC's that would block progress and give you new weapons. This demo actually had very little to do with the core concept with Snatchems being mainly placed around as an extra bonus goal. This was the point of biggest feedback we got at the time with our name and whole game identity being tied around these creatures its was odd that they weren't actually that important. In this early version the game was very linear and it was mainly a test for if the concepts of the game held up as well as feature tests for if the features we made were working properly. 

# First Revision 
The first major revision was making our play space larger with more branching paths we felt that early on with many designers we should make the game larger to match since there wasn't much to do with very linear levels. We also decided to add metroidvania collectathon to the GDD and high concept due to a potential investor suggesting us to add in some potential ones to make us more of a "blue ocean" and not compete with other perhaps similar games. I think this revision was one of the harshest reality checks because we spent a very high collective amount of time on this one without the best results. After the first play test we then continued to patch on Systems to try to fix underlying issues with the game. 

In this version you could pick a snatchem which would give you stat boosts and would follow you around. they could also be thrown at enemies and were overall more useful and part of the game than the previous version. At first we had a huge level with NPC's but the game didn't quite feel like a metroidvania, there were a multitude of issues, players would get lost, the linear pathing and clear direction was gone and ultimately snatchems still did not feel that great as a reward which was the case for many reasons. eventually we added set bonuses which increased the reward of finding snatchems we also made it so some mechanics were locked behind sets of snatchems. we ended up adding full descriptions, names, stat bonuses, set bonuses, in a attempt to get snatchems to be the focal point. we also added a quest system and quest tracker to get the players more on point and oriented. ultimately while very good systems that were great and would be great for most games they didn't fix the core issues and so we had to move on.

I think the biggest two lessons i learned are #1 during a pivot try to NEVER add scope the goal of a pivot is to prove a new system so it should be minimal #2 When a pivot doesn't land do not add stop gap measures to try and patch the pivot. 

Looking back these two decisions were very costly and ultimately wasted alot of development hours during a pivot its normal to have much of your work thrown into the shredder but you should never try to inflate that. i also think the two decisions compounded on each other we made a massive level which took a lot of time, by the time we were done sunk cost fallacy made us want to keep adding to make it good so we tacked on many features that were not entirely needed.  

# Second Revision 
The second revision was a complete overhaul of weapons and snatchems this iteration was probably my favorite but we ended up not going through with it due to it being difficult to balance and requiring lots of design effort it also made the game a bit too much of a game like vampire survivor hurting player agency alot and it went against the vision of a more skill based shooter game. 

How it worked was your snatchems were the guns and you would have a group of follower snatchems to pick up when you picked it up you could use it each snatchem had unique stats and handled slightly differently. when not in the hand you could throw them to create a secondary effect like an explosion or pushing the player back. when the snatchems were not in your hand they would have a greatly reduced fire rate and damage and would still automatically hurt enemies. This is probably the part where i worked the most on design and maybe thats why it was alot more personally enjoyable. though the actual gameplay was fun the team thought it was maybe best for a different game. looking back i think there was a few ways we could have made it work out but we ended up deciding to scope down a lot instead. 

# Final Demo
During the the final demo i moved off of doing design and was mainly doing art this version was a roguelike version of the game we ended on before stopping development. in this iteration snatchems were powerups. You had weapons that could be empowered by the snatchems and there were various weapons you could obrain . I mainly worked on the boss at the end of the demo as well as adding some visuals to enemies and levels.
